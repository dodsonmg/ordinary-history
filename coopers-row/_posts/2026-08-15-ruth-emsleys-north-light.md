Ruth Emsley leased 9 Coopers Row in 1902, having been drawn to the property specifically for the wide north-facing window of the former parlor — ideal, steady light for painting. She converted the room into a studio and, by several accounts, hosted informal weekend sketching sessions there for other local artists.

She left the house in 1919, and several of her landscapes from this period — including at least two believed to depict the house's own garden — are now held in a regional museum collection.
