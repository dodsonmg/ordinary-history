When Edward Voss took over 9 Coopers Row in 1834, the house's ground floor was reworked into a taproom, with the old cooperage workshop pressed into service as a storeroom for casks. He named the business the Thatcher's Arms, a nod to the family who had owned the house two generations before — whether out of genuine local memory or simple marketing is hard to say.

The pub operated under Voss and, briefly, a successor publican until the mid-1860s, when licensing records show the business wound down and the house reverted to residential use.
