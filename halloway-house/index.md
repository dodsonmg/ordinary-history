*1958–present*

Halloway House was designed in 1958 by the architect Rowan Fenwick as a private residence for Vivian Halloway, and remains one of the earliest and most technically ambitious examples of the New Brutalism in domestic architecture. Fenwick used the commission to push board-formed concrete further than it had been pushed in a house before — thin cantilevered slabs, minimal reinforcement cover, and an experimental aggregate mix chosen for its appearance rather than its long-term durability.

That ambition came at a cost. The house has needed near-constant attention since the 1970s, passing through a series of owners drawn to its raw, sculptural presence but not always equipped to care for it correctly. A well-intentioned repair campaign in 1986 did as much harm as the weather had. Since 2015 the house has been owned by the Modern Heritage Trust, a conservation organization that undertook a major stabilization and restoration effort to both repair the original 1958 fabric and undo the damage caused by the 1986 work.
