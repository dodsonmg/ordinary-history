By the time Gerald Priest bought Halloway House in 1983, the concrete's porosity had become an obvious problem — water ingress, staining, and the first visible spalling around the cantilevered edges. Priest, wanting to protect what he'd bought, commissioned a repair campaign in 1986 using the standard techniques of the day: a waterproof coating sealed over the exposed aggregate, and patch repairs in a modern, denser concrete mix wherever damage was visible.

The work looked, for a few years, like it had solved the problem. It hadn't. The coating trapped moisture that would previously have evaporated from the porous surface, and the denser patch material expanded and contracted at a different rate than the original concrete around it, opening new cracks at every patch's edge.
