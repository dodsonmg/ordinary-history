Vivian Halloway lived in the house that bears her name for sixteen years, from its completion in 1958 until 1974. Local reaction to the building was mixed from the start — neighbors reportedly called it "the bunker" — but Halloway was, by most accounts, unbothered, hosting architecture students and journalists alike for tours of Fenwick's work.

What eventually wore her down, she said afterward, wasn't the reception but the upkeep: hairline cracks appearing within the first decade, and a growing sense that the house's most striking feature — its raw, untreated concrete — was also its most fragile.
