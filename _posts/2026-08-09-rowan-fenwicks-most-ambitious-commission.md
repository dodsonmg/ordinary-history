When Vivian Halloway approached Rowan Fenwick in 1957, she reportedly gave him a single instruction: build something that had never been built before. Fenwick, then in his mid-thirties and still relatively unproven, used the commission to test ideas he'd only sketched until then — cantilevered concrete slabs thinner than most engineers of the period would have signed off on, and an aggregate mix chosen for the rough, almost geological texture it left on the exposed surface.

Construction took the better part of a year and, by Fenwick's own later account, several formwork failures the public never heard about. The house was completed in 1958 to considerable architectural press attention, and rather less enthusiasm from the structural engineers who inspected it decades later.
