The Modern Heritage Trust's restoration ran from 2016 to 2019 and split roughly evenly between two kinds of work: repairing genuine 1958-era deterioration, and undoing the 1986 repair campaign that had made much of that deterioration worse. Conservators removed the waterproof coating in tested sections, cut out and replaced the mismatched 1980s patch concrete with a hand-mixed match for Fenwick's original aggregate, and reinforced the most fatigued sections of the cantilevered slabs from within rather than adding visible external supports.

The goal throughout, per the Trust's published conservation plan, was fidelity to Fenwick's original design intent — including, where it could be safely retained, the very porosity that had caused so much of the building's trouble in the first place.
