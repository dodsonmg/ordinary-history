When the Modern Heritage Trust took on Halloway House in 2015, conservators found that a significant share of the work ahead of them wasn't repairing 1958 concrete at all, but removing Gerald Priest's 1986 repairs. The waterproof coating had to come off in sections, tested first to confirm the concrete beneath hadn't degraded beyond saving, and each of the denser patch repairs had to be cut out and replaced with a hand-mixed match for Fenwick's original aggregate.

Trust conservators later estimated that undoing the 1986 campaign accounted for close to half the total cost of the restoration — more, in the end, than repairing damage from thirty years of weather alone.
