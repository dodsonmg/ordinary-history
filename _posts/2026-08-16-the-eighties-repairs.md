Facing visible spalling and water staining, owner Gerald Priest commissioned a repair campaign in 1986: a waterproof coating applied over the exposed aggregate concrete, and patch repairs using a modern, denser concrete mix wherever damage had appeared. Both choices were standard practice for concrete repair at the time, but wrong for this particular building — the coating trapped moisture that the original porous concrete had been designed (deliberately or not) to release, and the denser patches expanded and contracted at a different rate than the surrounding original material, opening fresh cracks at nearly every repaired edge.

The damage this caused was not fully understood until the Modern Heritage Trust's 2016 survey, nearly thirty years later.
