Rowan Fenwick spent much of his later career defending the choices he made at Halloway House. The experimental aggregate mix that gave the concrete its distinctive rough texture also made it more porous than standard mixes of the period, and the thin cantilevered slabs he was so proud of left unusually little cover over the steel reinforcement inside.

In interviews from the 1980s, by then aware of the house's mounting maintenance problems, Fenwick maintained that the fault lay with decades of poor repair work rather than his original design — a position conservators who studied the building later would ultimately, if only partially, vindicate.
