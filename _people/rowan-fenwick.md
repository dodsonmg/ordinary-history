Rowan Fenwick was an architect associated with the early New Brutalism movement, known for treating raw, board-formed concrete as a finish material rather than something to be clad or rendered over. Halloway House, completed in 1958 for Vivian Halloway, is widely considered his most technically ambitious residential commission — he used it to test an experimental aggregate mix and unusually thin cantilevered slabs that he would go on to reference, and defend, for the rest of his career.
