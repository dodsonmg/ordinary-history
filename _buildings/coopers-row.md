Built around 1740 as a tradesman's dwelling with an attached workshop, 9 Coopers Row has been a private home, a public house, an artist's studio, and a subdivided tenement over the course of roughly three centuries — before eventually being restored to a single dwelling.

Thomas Thatcher, a cooper, bought the house in 1771 and added a rear extension for his growing dairy and workshop needs. After the Thatcher family's tenure ended, the house passed through a run of very different uses: decades as a public house known as the Thatcher's Arms, a spell as a painter's studio prized for its north light, and a period subdivided into flats during the 1930s and '40s — before Margaret Cole bought the whole property in 1946 and reunified it as a single home.
