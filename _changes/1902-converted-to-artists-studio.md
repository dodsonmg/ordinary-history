When painter Ruth Emsley leased the house in 1902, she had the former parlor's window enlarged to widen the north light for painting — the most significant physical alteration to the house's front elevation since the original build. The room served as her studio, and occasionally as an informal gathering space for other local artists, until she left in 1919.
