In 1834, Edward Voss converted the ground floor of 9 Coopers Row into a taproom, reworking the old cooperage workshop into a cask storeroom and opening the house to the public as the Thatcher's Arms. This marked the property's first shift away from purely residential use.

The public house operated for roughly three decades before the business wound down in the mid-1860s and the house reverted to a private dwelling.
