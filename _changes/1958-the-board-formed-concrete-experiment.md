Halloway House was built to a design that deliberately tested the limits of what board-formed concrete could do in 1958: unusually thin cantilevered slabs, minimal reinforcement cover, and an experimental aggregate mix selected for its rough, exposed texture rather than its proven durability. Formwork records that survive suggest at least two partial failures during the pour, quietly corrected before the press was invited to see the finished house.

The techniques Fenwick used here — many of them without prior large-scale testing — are the direct cause of nearly every conservation problem the building would go on to have.
